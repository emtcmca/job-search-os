What this engagement does

Bring intake, follow-up, and communication into one clearer operating model.

The goal is not to make the operation heavier. It is to make requests easier to route, status easier to see, documents easier to find, and communication more reliable under real portfolio pressure.

Who this is for

Teams balancing board requests, homeowner issues, vendors, and documentation at the same time.

This is usually the right fit for management companies, self-managed boards, and condo or HOA teams whose work enters through too many channels, whose service expectations are not steady enough, or whose follow-up depends too much on who happens to be holding the thread.

What is usually going wrong
  • Fragmented intake: board, homeowner, and vendor requests arrive through too many paths.
  • Murky prioritization: the team is not triaging common issue types cleanly enough.
  • Hidden vendor status: follow-up is happening, but not visibly enough for the full team.
  • Slow document retrieval: governing and operational references take too long to find when pressure is already high.
  • Memory-based execution: too much service context still depends on individual staff recall.
What I would deliver
Workflow summary

A clear picture of how requests, follow-up, and communication are moving today.

Intake and response model

A cleaner category structure with clearer response expectations by request type.

Follow-up visibility recommendations

Better structure for vendor issues, homeowner requests, and open loops.

Document and communication guidance

Recommendations for retrieval flow, recurring messaging, and practical consistency.

Technical depth when needed

I can also map where document intelligence, OCR, retrieval workflows, intake triage, or AI-assisted homeowner and board communication would strengthen the operation without overcomplicating it.

Platform familiarity

I have direct operating familiarity with the tools commonly sitting underneath this work, including CINC Systems, Buildium, AppFolio, Vantaca, and Frontsteps.

How the engagement runs

Focused enough to move quickly, detailed enough to respect the operating complexity.

Business days 1 to 2

Review intake flow, communication patterns, open-loop handling, document touchpoints, and the main operational bottlenecks.

Business days 3 to 5

Build the category model, follow-up structure, response guidance, vendor visibility approach, and document-handling recommendations.

Business days 6 to 7

Finalize the implementation path, templates, communication guidance, and any practical automation opportunities worth pursuing next.

HOA and condo operations get easier when communication, documentation, and follow-up are built into the workflow instead of living in partial notes and memory.
