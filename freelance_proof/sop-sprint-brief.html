What this engagement does

Turn process knowledge into something the team can actually use.

Good documentation should make the process easier to repeat, easier to teach, and easier to follow without pulling the same person back in every time the team needs an answer.

Who this is for

Teams that have process knowledge, but not yet a reliable way to use it.

This is usually the right fit when training is slow, handoffs vary by person, or the operation keeps relying on memory, shadowing, and informal explanations instead of a usable operating reference.

What is usually going wrong
  • Tribal knowledge: the process lives in people's heads instead of inside the operation.
  • Repeated questions: the same clarifications keep getting answered live because the docs are not usable enough.
  • Weak handoffs: ownership and completion rules are not obvious from the documentation structure.
  • Slow training: newer team members need too much interpretation help to work confidently.
  • Automation blockers: messy documentation makes later AI, retrieval, or workflow automation harder to trust.
What I would deliver
Structured SOPs

Readable process docs built for real operating use, not documentation theater.

Ownership and handoff notes

Clear guidance on who owns what and what completion actually means.

Training-ready formatting

References that work for onboarding, shadowing, and quick operational lookup.

Reusable template structure

A format the team can keep using as the documentation set grows.

Technical depth when needed

I can also organize the material so later AI lookup, retrieval, knowledge systems, and workflow helpers have a cleaner foundation, without forcing the documentation itself to sound technical.

How the engagement runs

Deep enough to make the documentation usable, focused enough to keep it moving.

Week 1

Review the current process, source material, existing references, repeated questions, and the main points of ambiguity or inconsistency.

Weeks 1 to 2

Draft the SOP structure, ownership notes, normal-path steps, handoff rules, exception handling, and training-ready reference format.

Weeks 2 to 3

Finalize revisions, quality checks, implementation notes, and the reusable documentation pattern the team can keep using.

Best-fit engagements

Best when the team needs documentation people will actually work from.

Best fit is a team that wants documentation people can work from immediately. This is not documentation theater or a policy binder that no one will touch after delivery.

Good SOPs do more than explain the work. They reduce ambiguity and take pressure off the people everyone keeps needing to ask.
