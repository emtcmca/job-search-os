What this engagement does

Turn workflow friction into a clear operating decision.

This work is designed to show where the workflow is failing under normal operating pressure, what needs to be cleaned up first, and where automation can help without turning the process into something harder to trust.

Who this is for

Teams that know the workflow is slipping, even if they cannot yet name every cause.

This is usually the right engagement for founder-led operations, small service businesses, and teams whose onboarding or service delivery feels different from client to client, whose details are being rebuilt from scattered notes, or whose work is moving forward without a clear handoff pattern.

What is usually going wrong
  • Scattered intake: setup details, approvals, and documents are being captured in more than one place.
  • Weak handoffs: ownership changes between people without a clean transfer point.
  • Uneven follow-through: timing and quality depend too much on whoever is handling the account that day.
  • Inconsistent client communication: the experience feels different from one onboarding to the next.
  • Hidden process knowledge: too much of the workflow still lives in one person's head.
What I would deliver
Current-state workflow summary

A clean picture of how the work actually moves today.

Friction and risk review

The main points of delay, inconsistency, and rework.

Quick wins and future-state outline

The cleanup moves worth making first and the structure they lead toward.

Automation and tooling map

The places where process automation, AI-assisted support, or better system use would make the biggest difference.

Technical depth when needed

If automation is part of the answer, I can also map prompt standards, routing logic, lightweight tooling hooks, knowledge-base structure, and where agent-style support makes sense while keeping human review in the loop where it should stay.

How the engagement runs

Structured enough to see the real workflow, focused enough to keep momentum.

Business days 1 to 2

Review the current sequence, source material, intake paths, and where the work enters, stalls, or gets rebuilt manually.

Business days 3 to 5

Map the friction points, clarify ownership, identify quick wins, and shape the improved workflow model around practical execution.

Business days 6 to 7

Finalize the cleanup sequence, recommendation priority, implementation notes, and any practical automation opportunities worth pursuing.

Best-fit engagements

A workflow audit should make the next move clearer, not just describe the mess in better language.
